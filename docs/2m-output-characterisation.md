# 2m output characterisation (CLK1, fundamental, with a cheap LC band-pass filter)

Measured 2026-10-31 (17:25-17:38) with the bench framework, firmware at commit `833b76c`.
Raw data: [`../tests-results/2m/2026-10-31T17-25-27__2m-lc-bandpass-137-140MHz-37dB.json`](../tests-results/2m/2026-10-31T17-25-27__2m-lc-bandpass-137-140MHz-37dB.json).
Setup: [`../tests/bench/setups/2m-lc-bandpass-137-140MHz-37dB.json`](../tests/bench/setups/2m-lc-bandpass-137-140MHz-37dB.json).
View it, and compare it with later runs, in the bench web UI (`python3 tests/bench/serve.py`, port 3333).
This is the 2m baseline: later runs with a different filter, amplifier or PLL set-up should be compared with it. The same
five tests and the same pad set as the 6m baseline ([`6m-output-characterisation.md`](6m-output-characterisation.md)) were used.

## Summary

| | Result |
|---|---|
| Filter -3 dB points | **112.2 to 153.8 MHz** (41.5 MHz wide, centred at 133 MHz), not a narrow 2m filter |
| 2m band (144-146 MHz) | inside the flat part: within about 0.2 dB of the passband level; ripple 2.4 dB overall |
| Filter rejection | only 10 dB at 99.6 and 161.4 MHz; 20 dB at 71 and 172 MHz; about 38 dB at 200 MHz |
| Wanted level leaving the filter | about **+3.6 dBm (2 mA) to +11.9 dBm (8 mA)** at 145 MHz (about +/-2 dB) |
| Close-in spurs, best | -63 dBc (2 mA, 144.6 MHz); -57 dBc (8 mA, 145.5 MHz) |
| Close-in spurs, median | -45 dBc (2 mA); -42 dBc (8 mA) |
| Close-in spurs, worst | **-38 dBc (2 mA) and -36 dBc (8 mA)**, both at 145.3 MHz |
| Strongest out-of-band emission | **725 MHz, the 5th harmonic: -37 dBc (2 mA), -42.5 dBc (8 mA)** |
| Other out-of-band | 140 and 150 MHz spurs, 5 MHz either side of the carrier: -54 dBc (2 mA), **-44 dBc (8 mA)**; 4th harmonic (580 MHz) -58 dBc and 3rd (435 MHz) -60 dBc at 8 mA |

The filter is the weak point. It removes little below 100 MHz or above 160 MHz, so it does not clean up the close-in or
5 MHz spurs, and it leaves the 5th harmonic at 725 MHz at -37 to -42 dBc.

## Setup

```
Pico W --I2C-- Si5351 (Adafruit, 25 MHz crystal, +151.3 ppm correction applied)
                 CLK1 --> LC band-pass, cheap import, marked "137M/140M"
                      --> T pad (27-33-27 ohm) --> BNC attenuator (marked "10 dB")
                      --> TinySA Ultra, low input
```

* CLK1 is on PLL A (shared with CLK0, but only CLK1 was enabled). The wanted signal is the fundamental.
* The filter is L-C-L-C-L in series, with more capacitors to ground at each junction (a five-element ladder). Its
  marking (137 and 140 MHz) is below the 2m band, but as the response shows, its passband is wide enough to include it.
* TinySA Ultra `tinySA4_v1.4-200` (HW V0.4.6), controlled over USB. Attenuation 0 dB, LNA off.
* The **pad set is the same two pads as the 6m baseline**, in the same arrangement: the T pad (15 dB) and the attenuator
  marked 10 dB (really 27 dB), together **37.4 dB at 145 MHz** (37.9 dB at 51 MHz); see the 6m document for how they were
  measured. All levels in the results are as read by the analyser; adding the pad back gives the level leaving the filter.

## Method

Unchanged from the other bands (see [`../tests/bench/README.md`](../tests/bench/README.md)); versions: drive_levels 1,
spur_map 1, spectrum_close 1, spectrum_wide 2, response 2.

1. **Filter response.** Carrier stepped across 60-200 MHz (0.25 MHz steps over 140-150 MHz, 1 to 2 MHz elsewhere) at 8 mA.
   The 100 to 113 MHz gap cannot be generated by the Si5351 and was skipped.
2. **Spur map.** 21 carriers from 144.0 to 146.0 MHz in 0.1 MHz steps, +/-2 MHz window, 401 points, 3 sweeps, at 2 and 8 mA.
3. **Close-in** and **wide spectrum** (5-790 MHz, RBW 100 kHz) at 2 and 8 mA, with 10 dB extra analyser attenuation to check
   whether a peak is real.
4. **Level against drive** at 145 MHz.

Reference point at the start and end: identical (drift 0.0 dB). The analyser saw up to -24.2 dBm in the 8 mA tests,
0.8 dB over the runner's -25 dBm limit (the runner recorded a warning). A separate check, below, shows the 8 mA close-in
spurs are not caused by this.

## 3. Out-of-band emissions (5-790 MHz)

The peak finder lists peaks more than 12 dB above the floor (about -101.5 dBm), so the detection limit is about
**-56 dBc at 2 mA and -64 dBc at 8 mA**.

| Frequency | 2 mA | 8 mA | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **725 MHz** | **-37.3 dBc** | **-42.5 dBc** | 5th harmonic of the carrier; real |
| 140 MHz | -54.3 dBc | -44.4 dBc | real; 5 MHz below the carrier |
| 150 MHz | -54.2 dBc | -44.9 dBc | real; 5 MHz above the carrier |
| 580 MHz | not seen | -57.9 dBc | 4th harmonic; real |
| 435 MHz | not seen | -60.0 dBc | 3rd harmonic, in the 70cm band; real |

The 725 MHz signal is also the strongest unwanted emission on the 70cm output (the 5th harmonic of the 145 MHz carrier
there), and for the same reason: nothing on the carrier side removes it. Here it is the 5th harmonic of the 2m signal
itself. The 140 and 150 MHz spurs grow faster than the carrier with drive (10 dB worse in dBc at 8 mA), as on 6m.

The steps are +4.8, +2.0 and +1.5 dB. The unfiltered source gives about +2.4 dBm at 2 mA at 145 MHz, so the filter's
insertion loss is small (within the +/-2 dB uncertainty of the pad correction).

## 5. Baseline for a PGA-103+ stage

From the datasheet (not measured here): gain about 25.3 dB at 145 MHz, output P1dB +21.5 dBm (at 400 MHz), maximum input
+21 dBm.

* At **2 mA (+3.6 dBm)** the output would be about +29 dBm if linear, well above the P1dB; at **8 mA (+11.9 dBm)** about
  +37 dBm. The amplifier would be heavily compressed at any of these drives, and compression makes harmonics worse.
* For a linear output, an input of about -5 dBm or less is needed, i.e. **about 9 dB or more of attenuation at 2 mA, and
  about 17 dB at 8 mA**, between the filter and the amplifier.
* The 725 MHz harmonic and the 140/150 MHz spurs would be amplified along with the wanted signal (the amplifier has 18.9 dB of
  gain at 725 MHz against 25.3 dB at 145 MHz, so the 725 MHz line gets about 6 dB better relative to the wanted signal).

## 6. What this suggests

0. **See section 7 first:** a low-pass plus the band-pass, with a short cable between them, does much better than either alone.
1. **Replace or add to the filter.** This filter is a 40 MHz wide band-pass: it hardly rejects the close-in spurs and
   leaves the 725 MHz harmonic at -37 to -42 dBc. A low-pass with a cutoff around 160 MHz would bring the 435, 580 and 725 MHz
   harmonics well down; spurs 1 to 5 MHz away need a narrower band-pass (a few MHz wide) than anything measured here.
2. **Choose the carrier frequency by its spurs.** 144.3 to 144.6 MHz is clean at both drives; 144.7 to 145.4 MHz, including
   145.0 MHz, is the worst.
3. **Use low drive and let the amplifier provide the power.** The 140/150 MHz spurs are 10 dB better at 2 mA, and the worst
   close-in spur is 2 to 3 dB better.
4. **Measure 144.450 MHz and the frequencies in use directly** before relying on them; they are not in the 0.1 MHz map.

## 7. Other filter set-ups

Three more 2m runs were made with the same five tests, the same pad set (37.4 dB) and the same cable and adapters; only the
filters changed. All are in `tests-results/2m/` and can be compared in the web UI.

* `2m-lpf-200MHz-37dB`: a cheap low-pass marked 200 MHz (three series inductors, four shunt capacitors) in place of the band-pass.
* `2m-lpf-200MHz-then-bpf-137-140MHz-37dB`: that low-pass, then the band-pass, joined directly.
* `2m-lpf-200MHz-15cm-bpf-137-140MHz-37dB`: the same two filters with **15 cm of coax between them**.

| | Band-pass only (baseline) | Low-pass only | Low-pass + band-pass, touching | Low-pass + band-pass, 15 cm apart |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Carrier at the analyser, 8 mA | -25.5 dBm | -28.0 dBm | -26.5 dBm | -26.5 dBm |
| Highest level the analyser saw | -24.2 dBm (over) | -26.9 | -24.4 (over) | -25.4 |
| Close-in spurs, 2 mA: worst / median | -38 / -45 dBc | -47 / -59 | -45 / -58 | -45 / -57 |
| Close-in spurs, 8 mA: worst / median | -36 / -42 dBc | -46 / -51 | -42 / -45 | -41 / -46 |
| 140 and 150 MHz spurs, 8 mA | -44 / -45 dBc | -47 / -46 | -46 / -47 | -46 / -47 |
| 2nd harmonic, 290 MHz, 8 mA | not seen | **-27 dBc** | not seen | not seen |
| 3rd harmonic, 435 MHz, 8 mA | -60 dBc | -52.5 | -60 | -57 |
| 4th harmonic, 580 MHz, 8 mA | -58 dBc | -58 | -62 | not seen |
| 5th harmonic, 725 MHz, 2 mA | -37 dBc | -43.5 | -36 | -52 (not verified) |
| 5th harmonic, 725 MHz, 8 mA | -42.5 dBc | -45 | **-34** | **-50** |

"Not seen" means below the detection limit (about -56 dBc at 2 mA and -64 dBc at 8 mA). The 725 MHz entries are partly limited
by pickup (see below).

### What the four runs show

* **The low-pass filters almost nothing in the 2m band's neighbourhood.** Its response is within about 1.2 dB from 60 to
  200 MHz, so its -3 dB point is above 200 MHz and it leaves the 2nd harmonic (290 MHz) at -27 dBc. The band-pass is what
  removes the 2nd harmonic.
* **The close-in spurs are 5 to 14 dB better in the runs with the low-pass in the chain** than with the band-pass alone
  (2 mA median -57 to -59 dBc against -45 dBc). I do not know why: the carrier changes by only 1 to 3 dB. It might be how the
  filter's reflections load the Si5351's output stage. It is a measured effect, not an explanation.
* **The 140 and 150 MHz spurs do not change with any filter** (-44 to -47 dBc at 8 mA): they are well inside every passband.
* **Two unshielded filters touching each other couple around each other.** With the band-pass directly on the low-pass, the
  725 MHz harmonic rose to -34 dBc (-60 dBm at the analyser), worse than either filter alone. Putting 15 cm of coax between them
  brought it back to -50 dBc (-76 dBm), 16 dB better, and cleared the 4th harmonic by 11 dB.
* **Best arrangement so far: low-pass, short coax, band-pass.** It removes the 290 MHz harmonic, takes the 435 to 725 MHz
  harmonics to -57 dBc or better, and keeps the close-in spurs at -57 dBc median at 2 mA. It cannot do anything about the
  close-in or 5 MHz spurs at 8 mA (-41 to -47 dBc).

### Pickup floor at 725 MHz

With the Si5351 running at 8 mA (145 MHz carrier) but no signal path through the filters, the analyser still sees 725 MHz:

| Arrangement | 725 MHz | 580 MHz | 435 MHz |
|---|---|---|---|
| Filter output terminated, pad set disconnected, its input open | -67.7 dBm | -87.6 dBm | at the floor |
| Same, but the pad set's input terminated with 50 ohms | **-81.4 dBm** | -91.0 dBm | at the floor |
| Output off | at the floor (-98 dBm) | | |

The first arrangement mostly measured an open connector acting as an antenna. The terminated figure is the real pickup floor:
**-81 dBm at 725 MHz**, which is about **-55 dBc** against the 8 mA carrier. Readings near that level, including the spaced
run's 725 MHz figure, are partly pickup from the unshielded board, cables and filters. The three earlier 725 MHz readings
(-68, -73 and -60 dBm) are 8 to 21 dB above it, so pickup does not explain the differences between those runs. A metal box
around the Si5351 board and its outputs would be needed to measure, or radiate, much below this.

## Limitations

* Four filter set-ups, one pad set, one board, room temperature (sections 1 to 6 are the band-pass baseline; section 7 compares the others). No amplifier or antenna was connected. The board, filters and pads are unshielded.
* The pad was measured with no filter in front of it; a mismatch with the filter adds an uncertainty of about 2 dB to the
  levels leaving the filter. The dBc figures do not depend on it.
* The 8 mA analyser level was marginal (up to -24.2 dBm, limit -25). The close-in spurs were checked as above and are real;
  the 725, 140 and 150 MHz out-of-band signals were checked in the wide scan.
* The spur mechanism on 2m is not confirmed: the 6m explanation (distance from an integer ratio) has not been shown to
  hold here.
* The TinySA is not a calibrated instrument: the relative numbers (dB, dBc) are more reliable than the absolute dBm.
* The 2m output was not tested through the SAW (the 70cm filter), nor was the interaction between outputs repeated with the
  new filters fitted.
